The [synthesis pipeline](/docs/synthesis/) maintains and grows the corpus from
what is already written. **Discovery** goes one step further: it surfaces facts
nobody wrote down, by inference, both forward (new consequences) and backward
(unstated keystones).

## Why retrieval can't find them

The most load-bearing facts are the *least* likely to be written down, because
they are so foundational that everyone assumes them. Retrieval finds what is
*similar* to your query, but a keystone underwrites facts that are **dissimilar
to each other**, so similarity never surfaces it. No tuning of similarity search
changes that. knomit reads the **shape** of the corpus instead: a mechanism named
by facts in two otherwise-unrelated clusters is where an unwritten premise
hides.

<BridgeDiscovery />

A **bridge** is a group of facts that name the same **motif** (the same
mechanism or failure shape) while sitting in *different* similarity clusters
(distinct Louvain communities over the embedding graph) and being about
different subjects. The shared mechanism is the signal that similarity missed.
A shared domain or entity name is not enough to make a bridge. Motif bridges are
found mechanically, without a model call. In a knowledge base written by many
agents, such a bridge can link what different agents learned in different areas.

A motif group becomes a candidate only when it passes three checks, in order:

- **Not generic.** A motif carried by more facts than the larger of 12 and 2% of
  the corpus has gone generic. It bridges nothing, and review reports it so the
  motif can be split.
- **Separated.** The members sit in at least two similarity clusters.
- **Different subjects.** The members are not about the same thing.

Candidates are then scored (see [the quality gate](#the-bridge-quality-gate))
and ranked by that score. Where two groups score the same, the one whose members
share more entities comes first; entity overlap only breaks ties, and domains
play no part.

Each candidate goes to one of two **lanes**. If at least one similarity edge
joins its members, it is a **near** bridge and runs forward. If none does, it is
a **far** bridge and runs backward: facts that look unrelated but share a
mechanism are where a keystone would sit.

The communities come from the cluster cache, whose Louvain parameters are
`[cluster_cache] resolution` (default `4.0`; a higher γ yields more, smaller
communities) and `min_community_size` (default `2`, below which a community is
relabelled as noise). Both have `KNOMIT_CLUSTER_CACHE_*` overrides; see
[Configuration](/docs/configuration/#config-backed-override-knomittoml).

## The two directions

Both directions are served by [`knomit_review`](/docs/mcp-tools/#knomit_review--maintain-the-kb),
which enumerates motif bridges over its ordinary seed pool.

| Direction | Shape | Lane | Produces |
|-----------|-------|------|----------|
| Forward | **consequence**: E follows from \{A,B,…\} but no single fact states it | near, at every effort | `synthesis` fact |
| Backward | **keystone**: an unstated premise E that, if false, invalidates \{A,B,…\} | far, at `high` effort only | `hypothesis` fact, gated by blast radius |

Both write `origin: discovered`. The boundary is deliberate: discovery adds a
direction to the existing `synthesis` and `hypothesis` types, never a new fact
type. `knomit_hypothesize` does not produce discovery items; at `medium` or
`high` it only reports that the entity and domain axis no longer qualifies a
bridge.

A discover item has a third outcome besides proposing and skipping. When the
agent finds that the corpus already states what it would have proposed, it
**reinforces** that fact: the seeds join its refs as an independent derivation,
with a one-sentence reason why the two claims are the same.

## The effort dial

`effort` (`normal` · `medium` · `high`) is a dial on the existing `review` /
`hypothesize` operations rather than a separate tool, and it doubles as a budget.

| Effort | Motif matching | Forward (near) bridges | Backward (far) bridges | Motif vocabulary passes |
|--------|----------------|------------------------|------------------------|-------------------------|
| `normal` (default) | exact | up to 2 | none | no |
| `medium` | exact | up to 4 | none | yes |
| `high` | exact, or two shared stemmed words | up to 8 | up to 8 | yes |

At `normal`, two facts must name exactly the same motif (after alias
resolution), so on a corpus without motifs discovery adds nothing. `high`
also groups motif names that share at least two stemmed words, so raising
effort only ever adds candidates. The caps apply to scoped and unscoped runs
alike, so a `high` run never attempts the whole corpus.

A call that omits `effort` falls back to
`KNOMIT_DISCOVERY_EFFORT_DEFAULT` (or `[discovery] effort_default`), default
`normal`. Set it to `medium` or `high` to make discovery the standing behaviour
of an endpoint rather than a per-call opt-in.

An optional **scope filter** (`domain` / `entities` args) bounds the seed pool;
empty means the whole corpus. A scoped run is **exempt from the synthesis
watermark**, so you can re-target discovery at one area without disturbing
unscoped runs. Discovery never feeds on its own output: `origin: discovered`
facts are excluded as bridge seeds.

## The bridge quality gate

Not every bridge is worth spending a work item on. Before a candidate reaches
the model, the scorer applies a gate that depends on its lane.

A **near** bridge is rejected outright unless all three hold:

- **Cohesion** ≥ `coh_floor` (default `0.5`): the fraction of member pairs
  joined by a `SIMILAR_TO` edge.
- **Separation** ≥ 2 distinct communities: a set inside one cluster is not a
  bridge. This is fixed in code, not configurable.
- **Members** ≤ `max_members` (default `5`): larger sets are gated out rather
  than scored.

Near bridges that pass are scored
`Q = w_coh·cohesion + w_gap·derivation_gap + w_spec·specificity` (weights all
default `1.0`) and kept when `Q ≥ quality_floor` (default `0.0`, which keeps
every gate-passing set). **Cohesion** is how tightly the members hang together,
**derivation gap** is the fraction of member pairs *not* already linked by
`DERIVED_FROM` (a set whose members already derive from one another states
nothing new), and **specificity** is the rarity of the shared motif: `1/df`
summed over every motif all members carry, so a motif on a large share of the
corpus contributes almost nothing, and a second shared motif adds to the score.

On a **far** bridge cohesion is inverted: the members must be dissimilar. The
cohesion floor does not apply there, while separation and the member cap still
do, and the score is
`Q = w_spec·specificity + w_gap·derivation_gap + w_coh·(1 − mean pair cosine)`,
kept under the same `quality_floor`. Both lanes' scores stay on one scale.

Tune the gate when discovery is spending its budget on candidates you keep
rejecting. Raising `quality_floor` above `0` is the blunt lever; the weights let
you say *why*: raise `w_spec` if the run keeps bridging on common motifs, raise
`w_gap` if it keeps re-stating things already derived. The scored components are
exposed by the calibrate tool, so a floor can be picked against a real corpus
rather than guessed.

These six keys are **TOML-only**: they have no `KNOMIT_*` environment
override, so a purely env-configured deployment cannot reach them. Defaults and
placement are in [Configuration](/docs/configuration/#knomittoml).

## Verification is model-less

There is no second adversarial model: the connected MCP agent is the sole
reasoner. Quality is enforced by a strict default-skip prompt plus a chain of
gates at ingest:

- `KNOMIT_DISCOVERY_CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD` (default `0.5`): the minimum
  confidence to write a proposal.
- `KNOMIT_DISCOVERY_BLAST_RADIUS_THRESHOLD` (default `1`, `0` disables): a
  backward keystone's anchor must transitively reach at least this many live
  dependents (transitive reverse-`DERIVED_FROM` count, live at HEAD). Forward
  proposals skip this gate.
- Embedding **dedup** against the corpus rejects a proposal already stated
  elsewhere.

`[discovery] bridge` in `knomit.toml` (`KNOMIT_DISCOVERY_BRIDGE`; `domain` ·
`entity` · `both`, default `both`) is a server-wide setting. It no longer
changes what is served, because a shared domain or entity does not qualify a
bridge; only a shared motif does.

## The origin axis

Discovery is one value of a fact's **`origin`**, the record of how it came to
exist, orthogonal to `type` and `kind`:

<Aside type="tip" title="origin: authored · distilled · discovered">
`authored` = written or asserted via `knomit_learn`; `distilled` =
synthesis-pipeline output; `discovered` = emergent, surfaced here. A discovered
fact is still a normal `synthesis` or `hypothesis` — `origin` only records that
knomit *inferred* it. Query the emergent set with `origin: discovered` on
`knomit_query`. See also [Concepts → Origin](/docs/concepts/#origin).
</Aside>
